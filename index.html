How the math works

  • Rolling watts = Crr × mass × 9.81 × speed. Crr for rubber flexing is fitted to the Continental GP5000 23/25/28/32 mm drum tests: it drops as pressure rises (roughly pressure−0.55) and as width rises (roughly width−0.38).
  • Bouncing follows Silca's breakpoint idea. Each width, load and road has a breakpoint pressure (rear tire carries 55% of the weight, breakpoint scales with load ÷ width, matching how Silca's recommended pressures fall with width). Above it, losses rise 4× to 25× faster than flexing losses fall, depending on how rough the road is, and a wider tire's larger air spring softens that rise. Rough roads also add a constant vibration loss that wider tires soak up better.
  • Tire aero = ½ × air density × CdA × speed³. The tires' CdA grows with width, plus an extra penalty once the tire is wider than the rim ÷ 1.05. Air density falls with elevation.
  • This is a simplified model. It shows the shapes and limits correctly, but your exact watts depend on the specific tire, rim and road. Use Silca's pressure calculator for the pressure you actually ride.
  • Sources: Bicycle Rolling Resistance GP5000 width test, Silca Part 4B: Rolling Resistance and Impedance, Silca Part 5: Tire Pressure and Aerodynamics, Slowtwitch: Tire Pressure and Rolling Resistance.
